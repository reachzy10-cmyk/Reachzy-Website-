REACHZY — WEBSITE, COPY & BRAND STANDARD

Working Source of Truth for Reachzy, ChatGPT, Codex and Future AI Systems

1. Purpose of this document

This document is the permanent standard for how Reachzy should be represented publicly.

It should be used whenever Reachzy's website, copy, positioning, UI content, creator pages, brand pages, marketing material, or related public-facing content is created, rewritten, reviewed, or redesigned.

The purpose is to prevent the project from drifting back toward generic AI-generated agency language, unsupported claims, unnecessary sections, or a visual/copy style that does not represent what Reachzy actually is.

This document is a rulebook, not merely a source of ideas.

When there is uncertainty, this document should take priority over an AI's assumptions.

Codex must read and follow this standard before making significant changes to Reachzy's website or public-facing copy.

The same applies to ChatGPT and other AI systems working on Reachzy.


---

2. What Reachzy is

Reachzy is an influencer marketing agency.

It works on both sides of the creator-brand relationship.

On the brand side, Reachzy helps identify suitable creators, approach creators, develop campaign opportunities, and help structure and manage collaborations.

On the creator side, Reachzy helps identify relevant sponsorship opportunities and presents them to creators so they can decide whether they want to proceed.

Reachzy should therefore not be represented as merely:

a creator directory

a lead-generation database

a talent marketplace

a cold-outreach service

a brand-only influencer agency

a creator-only sponsorship platform


The broader identity is:

Reachzy builds creator-brand collaborations around genuine relevance between the product, the creator and the creator's audience.

This wording may be improved for naturalness in final copy, but the underlying idea should remain.

The brand should communicate that Reachzy is concerned with whether a collaboration actually makes sense, rather than simply matching brands and creators because the numbers look attractive.

3. Core positioning

The central positioning should not be built around the word "fit".

"Fit" can be useful language, but it is not distinctive enough to become Reachzy's entire identity.

Reachzy should not repeatedly say:

fit

right fit

relevant

audience fit

perfect fit


in every section.

The actual concept behind those words is more important:

Does this collaboration make sense for both the product and the creator's audience?

That idea should appear naturally through examples, explanations, creator selection, campaign structure and proof.

Reachzy should feel like a company that understands why a particular creator makes sense for a particular product, rather than a company that repeatedly tells people that it cares about "fit".


---

4. Reachzy's two-sided model

The website must clearly communicate that Reachzy has two audiences.

For brands

Reachzy helps brands:

find suitable creators

approach creators

identify relevant campaign opportunities

structure collaborations

manage important campaign communication/processes where applicable

report or communicate campaign performance where applicable


The brand should leave the website understanding:

"Reachzy can help me find the right creator and turn that into an actual campaign."

For creators

Reachzy helps creators:

find relevant sponsorship opportunities

avoid having to chase every possible brand themselves

understand what an opportunity involves before deciding

maintain control over whether they accept an opportunity


The creator should leave the website understanding:

"Reachzy can bring me sponsorship opportunities that make sense for my content, and I get to decide what I take."

5. What Reachzy should NOT pretend to be

This is one of the most important rules.

The website must never make Reachzy look more mature, larger, more established, or more operationally complete than it actually is.

Do not invent:

large creator rosters

campaign volume

client counts

campaign success percentages

revenue

testimonials

brand logos

case studies

partnerships

contracts

payment infrastructure

reporting systems

dashboards

automation systems

operational guarantees


unless these actually exist and are approved for public presentation.

An AI must never fill gaps with plausible-sounding agency language.

When a capability is planned but not currently operating, it should not be presented as an established Reachzy service.

Reachzy currently has the strongest confirmed operational focus around:

creator discovery

brand discovery

creator-brand matching

campaign planning

creator outreach

brand outreach

developing sponsorship opportunities

Other services may become part of Reachzy's operation, but the website must not automatically claim them simply because they are normal for an influencer marketing agency.

Examples of things that require explicit confirmation before being presented as current capabilities:

contract management

payment handling

full production management

guaranteed reporting schedules

The current AI-generated plans contained several of these claims, including 48-business-hour proposals, one revision round, 30-day reporting, production management, and contract/payment handling. These are not automatically approved claims.  

The standard is:

Real capability first. Copy second.

7. Business model and pricing communication

Reachzy should not publicly explain its internal commission model as part of the general website positioning.

The current working business assumption is that brands pay for the creator/campaign, while Reachzy's compensation is taken from the creator side.

However, this is a working business decision rather than a permanent public claim, and it may change.

Therefore:

Do not use as standard website copy:

"No upfront fee"

"We only earn when..."

"We take a commission..."

detailed creator commission explanations

statements designed specifically to make the business model sound attractive


The current copy plan repeatedly used "No upfront fee". That should not automatically survive into the final site. 

The website should focus on:

What Reachzy does and why it is useful.

It does not need to explain internal economics unless there is a specific strategic reason to do so.

8. Creator positioning

Creator messaging is important and should feel genuinely creator-oriented.

The creator should not feel like:

inventory

an advertising placement

a database entry

a number attached to a campaign


The creator should feel like a person whose content, audience and reputation matter.

Reachzy should communicate three things:

1. Relevance

Reachzy should not send creators random opportunities simply because a brand wants promotion.

Opportunities should have a reasonable relationship with the creator's content and audience.

2. Time saved

Creators should not have to spend their time chasing every possible sponsorship opportunity.

Reachzy exists partly to reduce that burden.

3. Control

Creators should have the opportunity to understand the deal before deciding whether to proceed.

The creator's approval matters.

However, creator control should not be exaggerated into claims that Reachzy can guarantee a campaign.

The process still depends on:

the creator

the brand

the campaign requirements

communication

timing

commercial agreement


The website should therefore communicate creator control without implying:

"Once Reachzy brings you an opportunity, the campaign will definitely happen."

9. Creator niche

Reachzy should currently be positioned tech-first.

The primary creator categories are:

technology

productivity

software

SaaS

AI


The website should be able to expand later, but the current public positioning should not become so broad that Reachzy looks like a generic influencer agency covering everything.

Reachzy should still be able to work with other categories when strategically appropriate.

For example, a future campaign might involve a technology brand and a creator outside the narrowest definition of the tech niche.

The answer is not to make the website generic now.

The public identity should remain tech-first while leaving room to expand.


---

10. Brand positioning

The brand side of Reachzy should communicate several benefits simultaneously.

A brand should understand that Reachzy can help with:

discovering the right creators

finding creators whose audiences are relevant

reducing the time required to find creators

turning creator discovery into an actual campaign

structuring collaborations

helping manage the process where Reachzy is actually involved


However, performance and conversion should not become the entire sales pitch.

Reachzy should be interested in outcomes, but it should not promise that creator campaigns will automatically convert.

Avoid statements such as:

> "This creator will convert your audience."



Instead communicate the reasoning:

> the creator's audience already has a reason to care about this type of product



or equivalent natural language.

Performance can be demonstrated through real historical evidence, especially creator campaign results.


---

11. Proof philosophy

Proof is extremely important.

Reachzy should not try to look credible through empty statements such as:

"trusted by leading brands"

"industry-leading"

"high-performing creators"

"proven results"

"hundreds of creators"


unless those claims are actually supported.

The stronger approach is:

Show the evidence.

For creators, useful proof includes:

creator identity

channel

subscriber count

views

audience information where verified

sponsorship history

previous brands

historical campaign performance

campaign metrics

other verified information demonstrating why the creator is commercially relevant


Historical campaign results are particularly valuable because they help a brand understand:

This creator has already been sponsored before, and there is evidence behind that sponsorship history.

12. AI Learners India positioning

AI Learners India is currently the strongest creator proof available to Reachzy.

However, it should not dominate the entire homepage when the broader Reachzy identity is being established.

The current intention is:

Reachzy homepage = establish Reachzy

/for-brands = demonstrate creator credibility

/creators/ai-learners-india = detailed individual creator profile

/for-creators = explain the creator-side proposition


AI Learners India can therefore appear prominently on the For Brands page.

The homepage should not make it appear as though:

"Reachzy = AI Learners India."

The reason for using creator proof is to establish credibility, not to falsely imply a large roster.

Any information presented about the creator must be verified and accurate.

The existing copy plan contains detailed metrics and campaign evidence for AI Learners India. 


---

13. Campaign evidence rules

Creator sponsorship history and campaign performance can be used as strong proof.

But the wording must distinguish between:

the creator's historical campaign evidence

and

campaigns directly managed or created by Reachzy.

Do not turn a creator's previous sponsorship history into an implied Reachzy case study unless Reachzy actually managed that campaign.

For example, it is acceptable to communicate that a creator previously worked with a brand and generated certain verified results.

It is not automatically acceptable to imply:

> "Reachzy generated these results."



unless that is factually true.

This distinction is extremely important.


---

14. Case studies

At the current stage, Reachzy should not manufacture traditional case studies merely to make the website look more established.

A creator's verified sponsorship history can be used as proof.

That is different from claiming a formal Reachzy campaign case study.

When Reachzy eventually has genuine campaigns with measurable outcomes, those can become actual case studies.

Until then:

Do not fill an empty case-study section with invented content.

15. Writing tone

The desired Reachzy tone is:

Premium agency + professional + minimalist + creative.

It should feel appropriate for an influencer marketing agency.

The website should feel more like a carefully built professional brand than a startup landing page generated from a template.

The writing should be:

confident

intelligent

specific

professional

human

emotionally engaging

concise when possible

detailed when useful


It should not feel:

corporate for the sake of sounding corporate

overly polished

robotic

repetitive

over-explained

salesy

desperate

childish

generic

16. Story-driven writing

The website should have some narrative.

A visitor should feel that they are progressively understanding Reachzy rather than scrolling through a collection of disconnected marketing sections.

The experience should answer naturally:

What is Reachzy?

Then:

Why does it matter?

Then:

How does it work?

Then:

Why should I trust it?

Then:

What does this mean for me?

Then:

What should I do next?

The site should earn the visitor's attention.

The goal is not maximum information density.

The goal is:

The visitor spends several minutes exploring because the website is interesting and useful, not because the website forces them through endless sections.


---

17. CTA philosophy

Calls to action should appear where they make sense.

Do not put a large "Start a campaign" button at the very top simply because every marketing website does it.

The visitor should first understand enough about Reachzy for the CTA to feel logical.

CTA placement should be determined by the story of the page.

Primary actions should remain obvious, but they should not feel aggressive.

For brands:

Understand → trust → contact

For creators:

Understand → see relevance → contact


---

18. Homepage content philosophy

The homepage should provide a moderate amount of information.

It should not try to contain the entire business.

It should not be:

a five-line landing page

an enormous encyclopedia

a generic section stack


The homepage should establish:

1. what Reachzy is


2. why it exists


3. how it approaches creator-brand collaborations


4. that it works with creators


5. that there are two paths


6. enough proof to create trust


7. where the visitor should go next



Detailed information should live on the relevant pages.

The public structure should be clear.

/for-brands

/for-creators

Detailed explanation for creators.

As more genuine creators are added, individual profiles can follow the same structure.

20. Creator roster rule

The creator roster should represent real creators Reachzy is actually comfortable publicly presenting.

Do not create a roster of:

potential creators

scraped creators

researched creators

creators who have never interacted with Reachzy

creators merely found in a database


Discovery data is not the same thing as a public Reachzy roster.

The public site should communicate a relationship, not merely a search result.

21. Copywriting rules

This is a strict section.

Every public sentence should justify its existence.

A sentence should generally do at least one of these:

explain Reachzy

explain an actual service

establish credibility

provide useful information

move the visitor toward a reasonable action


Do not add sentences just to make a section appear longer.

Do not add filler.

Do not repeat the same idea in different words just to create additional sections.


---

22. Words and patterns to avoid

Avoid overuse of generic marketing language.

Particularly avoid repetitive use of:

relevant

great

powerful

seamless

strategic

innovative

transformative

end-to-end

impactful

meaningful

high-performing

game-changing

data-driven

tailored

cutting-edge


These words are not automatically banned.

The rule is:

Do not use them simply because they sound like marketing language.

Use concrete language instead.


---

23. Avoid AI-style symmetry

Do not repeatedly use constructions such as:

> Right-fit deals. Clear details. Your call.



> Different starting points. Same standard.



> Three steps. Your call at every one.



> Relevant. Clear. Creator-first.



These constructions can be effective occasionally.

Repeated across a website, they make the writing feel manufactured.

The current copy plan contains multiple examples of this pattern.

The writing should vary naturally.

Not every section needs a slogan.


---

24. Avoid unnecessary "not X, but Y" language

Avoid repeatedly explaining Reachzy through contrast:

> Not a directory.



> Not just an introduction.



> Not random opportunities.



One or two such statements may be useful.

A whole website built around negative comparisons becomes artificial.

Prefer explaining what Reachzy actually does.


---

25. Specificity beats polish

When choosing between:

a polished generic sentence

and

a slightly imperfect but specific sentence

prefer the specific sentence.

For example, instead of:

> "We create high-impact creator partnerships."



a more useful statement would explain:

> "We look for creators whose viewers already use the kind of tools your product is built for."



The exact wording can change.

The principle should not.

30. AI-generated copy review rule

AI-generated copy is a draft, never automatically the final answer.

Every AI-generated Reachzy page should be reviewed for:

1. factual accuracy


2. business accuracy


3. unsupported promises


4. unnecessary repetition


5. generic wording


6. AI-like patterns


7. tone


8. human naturalness


9. consistency with this document


10. whether every section actually deserves to exist



A copy detector can be used as an additional signal, but it is not the final authority.

The current website copy received inconsistent detector results, with multiple tools identifying strong AI characteristics while another gave a substantially lower estimate. That means detector percentage alone should not determine the quality of the copy.

The underlying writing quality matters more.


---

31. AI must not optimize for detector scores

Do not rewrite copy by simply replacing words until an AI detector produces a lower number.

That creates another form of artificial writing.

Instead:

Write naturally.

The goal is not:

> "Make this undetectable."



The goal is:

> "Make this sound like Reachzy was written by a real founder who understands the business."

responsive behavior

business claims

33. When Codex encounters missing information

If something is unknown, Codex should not guess.

It should use one of these approaches:

A. Ask for clarification.

B. Leave the element out.

C. Use an explicitly marked placeholder during development.

It should not invent a plausible answer.

This rule is particularly important for:

pricing

creator relationships

campaign history

operational capabilities

campaign metrics

legal claims

contracts

payments

guarantees

customer logos

34. Source hierarchy

When different sources conflict, use this order:

1. Current owner-approved Reachzy standard

This document.

2. Explicit current instruction from the Reachzy owner

A newer explicit decision should override an older assumption.

3. Verified business information

Actual operational facts and approved creator/campaign information.

4. Approved copy/design plans

Useful drafts, but subordinate to the rules above.

5. AI suggestions

Ideas only.

AI-generated material should never outrank actual Reachzy decisions.

35. What future Reachzy copy should accomplish

A successful page should make the visitor think something close to:

Brand visitor

"I understand what Reachzy does."

"I understand why they selected this creator."

"They seem to understand the audience, not just the follower count."

"I know what Reachzy can actually help me with."

"I have enough information to contact them."

Creator visitor

"I understand why Reachzy contacted creators like me."

"They aren't just sending random opportunities."

"I understand what information I would receive."

"I remain involved in the decision."

"I can contact them when something looks relevant."

These are the intended reactions.

Do not force these reactions through exaggerated claims.

Build them through clarity and evidence.

36. Overall Reachzy identity

Reachzy should feel like a small, serious, selective influencer marketing agency that understands technology creators and the brands that want to reach their audiences.

It should not pretend to be an enormous agency.

Its credibility should come from:

understanding the niche

selecting creators carefully

understanding audiences

knowing what brands are actually looking for

showing real creator evidence

communicating clearly

presenting a professional experience


The website should make a visitor think:

"These people actually understand this market."

rather than:

"This website has a lot of marketing words."


---

37. Final rule

When there is a choice between making Reachzy look bigger and making Reachzy look more credible, choose credibility.

When there is a choice between making copy sound impressive and making it sound true, choose truth.

When there is a choice between adding another section and making an existing section better, make the existing section better.

When there is a choice between generic agency language and a specific explanation of how Reachzy actually works, choose specificity.

When there is a choice between AI-generated assumptions and verified Reachzy information, choose verified information.

The standard in one sentence:

Reachzy should present real creator-brand expertise, real evidence and real capabilities through clear, human, premium communication — without pretending to be something it is not.
